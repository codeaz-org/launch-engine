FIRST COMMENT (post right after submitting):

Hi HN, I'm the maker.

On a team spread across enough time zones, any standup time is late at night for someone.
Written standup bots fix the timing but get one-line answers; Loom videos have the
opposite problem: lots of context, nobody watches twelve of them.

Asincly is the middle: each person gets a check-in window in their own morning, records
about two minutes (camera or screen + camera), and the AI drafts yesterday / today /
blockers from it. It uses your previous plan, so it ticks off what you said you'd do and
carries the rest forward, and it tags teammates you mention. You edit and send. Teammates
read a one-screen digest in their own morning and only tap the video when they need it.

Some things HN might care about:

- AGPL-3.0, self-hostable with Docker Compose; self-hosted has every feature, no license key.
- Next.js 16, Postgres with row-level security on every request (the app connects as a
  non-superuser role), transcripts and AI drafts encrypted at rest, media goes browser →
  bucket via pre-signed URLs and never through the app server.
- AI is pluggable: Groq (Whisper + Llama) by default; it works with AI off, people just
  type the check-in.
- Time zones: schedules are RRULEs with a window in each member's local time, DST-safe.

There's a hosted version (free up to 3 people) if you don't want to run it.

I'd love feedback on two things: does the "record, then read a summary" flow actually
beat a written standup for your team, and what would stop you from self-hosting it?
